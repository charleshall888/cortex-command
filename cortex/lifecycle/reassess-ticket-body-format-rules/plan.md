# Plan: reassess-ticket-body-format-rules

## Overview

Three independent edits plus a landing check. Two of them retire the dead citation ban: delete the unenforced rule from the shipped `backlog-author` skill, and correct the stale test-module docstring that concealed its death for months. The third scopes deletion bias — `cortex/requirements/project.md`'s **Deletion bias** paragraph gains the build-failure discharge rule that keeps the presumption of removal from attaching to a machine-consumed surface merely because its value is unmeasurable, with `## Why`/`## Role` as the worked example. No section of the five-section template changes, so `decompose.md`'s ADR-0007-backed lockstep prose dependency and the `load_parent_epic` extraction chain are untouched.

## Outline

### Phase 1: Retire the dead citation ban (tasks: 1, 2, 4)
**Goal**: Remove the citation rule that no gate has enforced since `e3aef4e5`, correct the docstring that hid it, and confirm the plugin mirror tracked the change (spec R1, R2, R5).
**Checkpoint**: `SKILL.md` still names all five sections and carries no citation rule; `tests/test_backlog_author.py`'s docstring names exactly its three extant tests; the mirror is byte-identical to canonical and the size/ratchet suites are green.

### Phase 2: Scope deletion bias (tasks: 3)
**Goal**: Record in the canonical statement when the presumption of removal attaches, what discharges it, and how long the discharge lasts — with the `## Why`/`## Role` retention as the worked example (spec R3, R4).
**Checkpoint**: The Deletion bias paragraph reads as one continuous paragraph, states the rule in the correct polarity, cites `#375` and `load_parent_epic.py`, and has grown by ≤6 sentences.

- **`tests/test_backlog_author.py` collects exactly three tests and its module docstring names exactly those three**, with no phantom entries and no scaffolding sentence describing a finished build step.

## Risks

- **Task 3's prose is the load-bearing artifact and no string match can check its polarity.** Critical review constructed an inverted restatement of the discharge rule that passed every token check the spec then carried — same citations, same vocabulary, opposite meaning. Task 3's greps confirm the citations landed; they cannot distinguish the rule from its inverse. The Acceptance read is the only real guard, and if it is skipped this lifecycle can land a canonical statement that says the opposite of what it intends and looks green doing it.
- **Retiring the citation ban changes shipped behavior in every consumer repo, not just this one.** `backlog-author` lives in always-installed cortex-core (ADR-0016), so the change ships unconditionally wherever cortex is installed. The case for removal rests on **zero enforcement since `e3aef4e5`**, not on a demonstrated null: corpus measurement was underpowered and confounded (cells of 17–29, contradictory signs across cortex-command and wild-light), so "no measurable effect" here means "cannot detect," not "proven harmless to remove." Revisitable in one commit if wrong, which is why the weak evidence is acceptable rather than blocking.
- **The ≤6-sentence cap on the project.md addition bounds count, not bytes.** `project.md` loads on every Clarify, so a faithful-but-dense addition can satisfy the count and still charge every future session more than the rule is worth. Compound run-on construction is the specific failure mode; prefer plain phrasing over mandated vocabulary.
- **`## Integration` and `## Edges` are deliberately left alone.** Under the rule Task 3 records, they have no consumer that fails on their removal and no filed bug recording harm, so their presumption of removal stands and they remain legitimately deletable. This plan does not act on that, and the resulting asymmetry — a newly written rule whose most obvious application is left unexecuted — is intentional. Acting on it is separate work with its own evidence bar.
