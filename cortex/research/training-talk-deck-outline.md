## What rev 21 changed and why (the harness becomes a decision diamond, 2026-07-27)

Presenter, on the rev-20 build: *"The icon is still not good for harness."* Third attempt, and the first one built on a diagnosis rather than a redesign.

**Why the first two failed — the reusable finding.** Every other object on this slide matches something the room already has a word for: the sheets are papers, the slab is a monolith, the window is a meter. **The harness matched nothing.** A rectangle with horizontal lines in it is the residual shape you draw when you haven't decided what the thing *is* — the empty set of iconography. Everything else was downstream of that: with no object, the choosing had to happen *inside* a generic frame; inside a frame it can only be shown as **state** (lit rows against dim ones); state rides on **brightness**; and brightness is the first thing a projector at twenty feet destroys. Two aggravating factors: the rows were drawn in the diagram's own connector vocabulary (3px horizontal strokes against 2px arrows), so they read as *more chain* rather than as content; and the count had no antecedent — one arrow in, four rows from nowhere, so "some of **what**" was never established.

**The fix.** A **flowchart decision diamond** — the one glyph this room parses pre-attentively, and a silhouette that *is* a noun. Four ochre strands fan in from `x=234` to the left vertex; the node is a diamond at `280–332 × 94–146`; two survivors leave the right vertex and carry **their own arrowheads** into the window at `x=436`; two discards curve down from the bottom vertex, in grey, with no head, ending in open space. The ochre continuity from the sheets supplies the missing antecedent, and **the only place on the slide where ochre turns grey is the moment of rejection**. Reached-vs-didn't is now geometry, so it survives any projector and any colour deficiency — that is the whole fix.

**The bracket makes it free.** Rev 20's habits bracket ends at `x=402`. The survivors are the only elements that cross it; both discards die inside (`x=350`, `x=332`). So the picture says, at no cost in elements: *some of your material leaves your half and reaches the window, the rest never leaves your half at all* — precisely what `#2.5` exists to cut.

**A deletion falls out of it.** The old `arrow(406, 436)` harness→window connector is gone; the survivors carry their own heads, so "two of the four *reached* the bar" is drawn rather than implied. `pt-arrow` drops from 3 to 2.

**The shrink beat got a better claim.** `#2.4` was `scaleY(0.66)` on a box, which only said *the box got shorter* — a size claim about an object rather than a claim about its job. Now the fan, node and discards scale `scaleY(0.55)` about the node's own centre (`transform-box: view-box; transform-origin: 306px 120px`) while the **survivors sit outside the scale group and do not move**, and the discards fade `0.5 → 0.22`. The diamond flattens to a lens and the fan collapses toward the axis: *there is less sorting left to do*.

**Honest accounting:** element count goes 9 → 11. This is a redistribution from interior detail into silhouette, not a reduction, and should not be sold as one. **Applied preemptively from the proposal's own self-objection:** converge-then-diverge is also the glyph for a network *hub*, and a hub forwards rather than drops — so the discards were shortened to quit inside the harness's own footprint rather than trailing toward a phantom second destination.

**THE CONTRAST FLOOR — the most valuable finding of the round, and it is a standing rule now, not a note about this icon.** A third proposal measured the palette against the background and found `--rule` (#2a2e3a) on `--night` (#0c0f16) is **1.42:1**. That is what *both* rejected rectangles used for their dropped rows. On a projector they were invisible: on the wall, v1 and v2 each read as "a box with two ochre lines in it" — **the icon has never once been seen doing its job**, which reframes both rejections as partly a judgement of something that wasn't reaching the room. The diamond had quietly reintroduced the same defect at `#2.4`, fading the discards to `0.22` = **1.52:1** — they did not dim, they vanished, silently breaking the deck's own "nothing sheds to zero" rule while appearing to honour it in the CSS. Fixed: discards now `0.62` (**4.2:1**) falling to `0.42` (**2.5:1**). Measured values for the palette: `--rule` 1.42:1 · `--parch-faint` 3.70:1 · `--parch-dim` 9.26:1 · `--ochre` 9.33:1 · `--parchment` 15.09:1. **`--rule` is for structure the room is not meant to read; anything carrying meaning needs `--parch-faint` or brighter.**

**Why the other two proposals lost, recorded so the ground isn't re-covered.** A *slotted sliding gate* (10–12 nodes, lanes interrupted at a hard grey edge) was rejected on its own self-objection: a slotted plate reads as a **filter** — fixed holes, fixed criteria — and the harness's whole job is that it re-decides every turn. A *five-line minimum* (four ochre lanes, one grey stop bar, 5 nodes — genuinely the most elegant construction of the three, with a shrink that has a real consequence) was rejected because it is geometrically an **improvement on v2** rather than a departure from it: rows terminating against a barrier is the family that has now been rejected twice, and it does not answer the diagnosis, since a bar and some lines still give the harness no noun. Its own objection conceded the risk — drawn only where it blocks, it reads as an obstacle, which turns `#2.4` into "gets out of the way". **Considered and deliberately not taken from it:** matching the fan's pitch to the sheets' rules so the lanes read as those rules continuing. It is a good idea, but the fan's *spread* is what makes "converge on a decision" read, and flattening it to the sheets' 12-unit pitch would trade the core gesture for a second, weaker antecedent — ochre continuity already supplies that one. **Independent convergence worth noting:** two of the three proposals separately said to delete the harness→window connector and let the surviving lanes be it. That deletion was already made; two agents arriving at it independently is good evidence it was right.

Validated headless: 7-beat state sweep, element inventory (`pt-fan`/`pt-node`/`pt-keep`/`pt-keep-head`/`pt-drop` present; `pt-box`/`pt-row`/`pt-stop` gone), cue coverage 7/7, 0 page console errors, nothing clipped at 1280×720, and a computed contrast pass on every non-ochre mark. deck.css `?v=41`, deck.js `?v=43`, notes.js `?v=39`. **Owed: presenter eyeball, rehearsal re-time.**

## What rev 20 changed and why (the caching claim gets honest, and the habits become a bracket, 2026-07-27)

Two presenter corrections on the rev-19 build of §2.

**The caching picture was overclaiming.** Presenter: *"the prompt caching is not entirely accurate. It isn't able to cache 100%."* Correct, and the mechanism is sharper than the note: caching is a **prefix match up to a `cache_control` breakpoint** (max 4 per request), with a per-model minimum cacheable prefix — so there is always a tail that is re-read at **full price even when it is byte-identical**, and cache reads are ~0.1× base input price rather than free. Rev 19 hatched most of the window fill and left a bright "new" sliver, which quietly asserted the opposite: *everything that didn't change is cached, and the only thing you pay for is new*. Rev 20 drops the "new" sliver entirely, shortens the hatch, and marks where reuse stops with a **drawn edge — the breakpoint, shown rather than described**. Caption → **"it all goes again every turn — only the unchanged start is reused"**, which is verbatim-consistent with §5's existing on-screen line ("the unchanged start is cached — ~10× cheaper to re-read"). §2 still makes no price claim; §5 keeps the economics. Net: one fewer element, one fewer label, and the claim is now true.

**The habits became a bracket over two parts.** Presenter: *"the habit is kinda confusing. I feel like habits is more like how you use the harness and the skills. The habits wraps both of those."* This is a better model than either previous attempt, and it explains why both failed: rev 18's backward arc and rev 19's head-of-chain step each implied a **single hand-off point**, when the real relationship is that two of the four parts are *yours* and a habit is what you do with them. So the habits are now an **ochre bracket spanning *what you write* and *the harness***, stem and label above it: **"your habits" / "how you use these"** — the presenter's own sentence, compressed. A habit is not a stage in the pipeline and not an artifact in it, and a bracket is the one shape that spans two things while belonging to neither. It is also the deck's established grammar (an earlier §2 cut used a brace to gather and name a group, and the presenter ruled at the time that an on-screen bracket makes a relationship structural rather than vocabulary drift). Label goes **plural** to match the deck's thesis ("Habits shift. The game doesn't.") and the protected dawn closer ("the habits are what you own"). The bracket lands in the same beat your files thin, so the shrink attribution from rev 19 is unchanged: `#2.4` the model grows and the harness thins (not your doing), `#2.5` the habits close and what you wrote gets shorter (yours). Removing the head-of-chain step let the viewBox revert to `0 0 780 240` and the stage to `min(66vw, 740px)` — the picture is bigger on screen than it was in rev 19.

Validated headless: 7-beat state-class sweep, element inventory (`pt-brace` / `pt-brace-stem` / `pt-cache-edge` present; `pt-loop` / `pt-new` gone), per-beat caption text, 0 page console errors, no reflow, no overflow at 1280×720. deck.css `?v=38`, deck.js `?v=41`, notes.js `?v=38`. **Owed: presenter eyeball, rehearsal re-time.**

## What rev 17 changed and why (§2 from anatomy to practice, 2026-07-27)

The presenter's redirect, verbatim: *"So maybe it should be 'The model didn't change. The process did'. Maybe you set out 3 fishing poles instead of 1, you remove the bobber, you figure out where best to drop the lines before dropping the lines."*

**The instinct behind it was right.** Rev 16 was clear but inert — a labeled diagram in a talk about what to do differently on Monday. Verbs teach better than nouns in this slot.

**What made the redirect safe to take.** A structural review argued §2 must remain the definition scene, on the grounds that the following 15 sections assume "harness = what you have/built, model = what you rent" as a stable split, and that dropping it would leave the sharpest heckle ("if the harness excludes the model, what are the next 15 sections managing?") with nowhere to live. **Verified false.** `grep` shows "harness" appears nowhere in `index.html` or `notes.js` outside §2's own headline and its own two cues, and §2 was created the same day; the deck ran fourteen revisions without it and every later scene was authored before it existed. The definitional debt was approximately zero. The same check applies to planting "habit" for the dawn closer — a same-day invention, not a dependency. **Lesson worth keeping: when an agent argues a scene is load-bearing, check when it was born and who actually references it.**

**The design.** Three rods set in a **fan** above a drop-off that runs *diagonally* across the water. The fan is evidence, not decoration: three evenly-spaced rods could never all reach a diagonal seam, so the geometry itself proves someone aimed — "I chose where these go" costs no words, no second picture, and no beat of its own. The bobbers hold every hook at one uniform shallow depth; taking them off is what lets the three hooks settle onto the seam at three *different* depths. One event therefore does double duty: it lands the disposal claim AND resolves the whole image, and the differential depths are the proof that the lines were aimed. Nothing is deleted — the bobbers rest faint on the bottom, because *you need no gear at all* is not the claim.

**What was deliberately NOT taken from the redirect.** "Figure out where best to drop the lines before dropping them" gets no beat and no on-screen claim. §10 The Crossing is built entirely around that reveal — *"Before casting off: sight the spot, write the bearing down"* → *"the miss was decided back at the dock — an angle too small to see"* — and stating the thesis seven sections early would turn §10's discovery into a rerun. This is the rev-13 precedent (a whole scene was cut for teaching one idea at three altitudes) applied prospectively. §2 *shows* deliberate placement; §10 still gets to *explain* it, with its own reveal intact.

**Wording.** "The process did" was rejected on register. The deck's vocabulary for what you control is always a thing or a behavior — harness, habit, workspace, tools, memory, instruction files — never an institutional abstraction; "process" also carries consultant baggage the no-jargon rule exists to keep out. Decisively: the protected dawn closer pairs rented *models* against owned *habits* — a rented thing against a personal behavior — so an institutional-register headline would force the audience to re-translate at the close, 33 minutes later. Replaced with **"The model didn't change. My half did."**, which reuses a phrase already in the talk track ("it's the half you actually control"). Sub-line → "the rod's theirs — what I do with it is mine". Thesis **"The gear comes off. The habit stays on."** → **"The rig changes. The habit stays on."**: the old line describes disposal only, which covers one of the three actions, while "the rig changes" covers placement, count and gear together.

**Known accepted risk.** Three rods nine sections before pv-2's proverb *"you set more lines"* may soften that line through familiarity, and the finale's three parallel windows are the deck's designated payoff for multiplicity. Mitigated by keeping §2 wordless on the subject — nothing on screen says "three", "lines" as a concept-word, "parallel", or "spawn" — so §4 and §17 still get to be the first to *name* it and the audience gets recognition rather than repetition. Confirm at rehearsal; this is the one thing a live read could overturn.

**Downstream:** §13's callback "that's the leader coming off" → "same as the bobbers coming off". Includes: deck.css `?v=32`, deck.js `?v=34`, notes.js `?v=33`. On-screen elements: 5.

## What rev 16 changed and why (§2 rebuilt for comprehension, 2026-07-27)

The presenter's verdict on the rev-15 build: *"a bit hard to follow... we may be leaning a little too hard into the fishing metaphor which may be losing the key messages because there is so much to comprehend."* A four-angle review followed (comprehension audit · cold reader given only the markup · subtraction redesign · wording pass).

**The metaphor was mostly a scapegoat.** The audit counted ~20 distinct items in a 55-second slot — 7 gear nouns, 6 metaphor mappings, 4 claims, and 4 notation grammars (brace-as-set-exclusion, in-place label crossfade, shed-and-fade, depth transform) none of which are reused from an earlier scene — against a same-slot norm of ~4-5. A 4-5x overshoot. Only ~15% of the difficulty was apportioned to fishing vocabulary; ~30% to payload count and ~30% to mapping non-obviousness. The audit also identified a structural tax unique to this scene: §2 is the **first** scene to build its visual vocabulary from zero, so every beat pays twice — acquire-the-noun and absorb-the-claim — with no amortization runway. §5 and §15 reuse objects §3 already taught and therefore spend their whole beat budget on one idea each.

**The cold reader's findings** (working from `index.html`/`deck.js`/`deck.css` alone, before reading any intent): payload 3 did not land at all — it was 100% spoken with no on-screen trace; payload 2 landed *backwards*, because shedding gear to 15% opacity reads as discarding while the actual claim is that dead scaffolding keeps charging rent; the callout labels rendered at full parchment while the rod and line rendered dim, so the eye read a bright word-list before matching any word to its picture element; b1 fired five simultaneous changes across two screen regions; the fish and the hook were drawn but never labeled or referenced. Its single one-sentence verdict: **"the habit" is coined in its own punchline** — the thesis points back at something that was never on screen.

**The fixes.**
1. **The waterline replaces the brace and the whole callout column.** Everything above the surface was either handed to you or clipped on by you — that is the harness. The line is the one thing that crosses the surface and keeps going — that is the model. The boundary already exists in the world, so it costs no geometry and introduces no notation to decode. A full-bleed ochre gradient fading upward marks the region without spending a word (a first pass used a flat rect and read as a UI panel — the gradient reads as light lying on the water).
